What actually makes IPTV cheap or expensive

The biggest cost for any IPTV provider is bandwidth at peak time. One HD stream uses roughly 2–4 GB an hour and a 4K stream several times more, multiplied across every subscriber watching the same Saturday-night fight. Providers that price far below the market can only do it by overselling capacity. You don't notice on a quiet weekday, but you do in the 90th minute of a final. Support costs money too: a 24/7 human on WhatsApp is not free.

That's why the real comparison isn't the sticker price. It's price per month you actually get to watch. A $2 service that buffers through every big game, or disappears after four months, is more expensive than it looks.

"Lifetime IPTV" deals: why they rarely last

A lifetime IPTV subscription (also sold as "IPTV à vie" in Québec) asks you to pay once for a service whose costs recur every month. The maths only works if the seller expects to disappear, rebrand, or cut your quality later. Common outcomes are a service that stops working within months, a "lifetime" that turns out to mean the lifetime of the reseller's panel, or a forced "upgrade fee". We don't sell lifetime plans for this reason. The yearly plan at $4.08/month is the honest version of a long-term deal.

Don't confuse lifetime IPTV with a lifetime app licence. Paying once for a player like TiviMate Premium is normal, because the app is software, not a stream. See TiviMate Premium.
